Part 1 · Discovery

Explain your role → map your work → find repetition → identify friction → select an opportunity.

Prompt 0Set up

Set some context

Purpose Let's give Copilot our expectations for how we want to work together.

For this conversation, I’m taking part in an interactive workshop and I want to work with you, rather than have you do everything for me. Please keep your responses short, concise and conversational. Give me just enough information to take the next step, rather than a complete or exhaustive answer. Throughout this conversation: Keep responses brief and easy to scan. Prioritise practical next steps over lengthy explanations. Don’t give me everything at once. Break complex tasks into small steps and work through them with me. When useful, ask me one question at a time to understand my needs or refine the result. If I give you feedback or change direction, adapt rather than restarting from scratch. If I want more detail, I’ll ask for it. Treat this as a collaborative working session. Help me think, not just give me answers.
Workshop steps
  1. Open a new Microsoft 365 Copilot chat
  2. Paste the prompt into Copilot
Prompt 1Discovery

Help Copilot understand my role

Purpose Give Copilot enough context to start, without writing a perfect role description.

I want to understand how Copilot could help me work more effectively. Start by helping me describe my role and the work I do by analysing my emails, teams chats, meetings and other data across my tenancy. Once you’ve looked at that and come up with a starting assumption ask me one question at a time about my responsibilities, the people I support, the outcomes I am responsible for and the types of work I regularly complete. Make sure the questions are related to assumptions you’ve made so you can refine the view. Once you have enough information, summarise my role and ask me to correct anything you have misunderstood.
Workshop steps
  1. Paste the prompt into Copilot
  2. Answer the questions
  3. Review the final summary
  4. Correct anything missing or inaccurate

Tip: Don't worry about a polished job description — you want a practical picture of what you actually do.

Prompt 2Discovery

Map my regular work

Purpose Move from a broad role description to specific activities and workflows.

Using what you now know about my role, help me map the work I regularly complete. Ask me questions about my daily, weekly, monthly and occasional activities. You can also compile this based on what you already know. Include communication, meetings, planning, reporting, creating content, reviewing information, supporting other people and administrative work. Then organise my work into clear categories.
Workshop steps
  1. Run the prompt in the same chat
  2. Check the categories Copilot creates
  3. Add anything Copilot missed

Tip: The rest of the discovery depends on this map being realistic, so add anything Copilot missed.

Prompt 3Discovery

Find repetitive work

Purpose Identify work that happens repeatedly and follows similar steps.

Review the work we have mapped and help me identify repetitive activities. Look for tasks I complete frequently, requests that follow a similar pattern, content I recreate, information I repeatedly search for and processes where I use the same steps each time. Ask me follow-up questions where needed. Then list the repetitive activities and explain what makes each one repetitive.
Workshop steps
  1. Run the prompt
  2. Answer the follow-up questions
  3. Review the repetitive activities listed

Tip: Stuck? Ask yourself: What do I copy and paste regularly? What do people repeatedly ask me? What do I recreate each week or month? What process lives mainly in my head?

Prompt 4Discovery

Find friction and effort

Purpose Examine where effort, delay and frustration occur.

Using everything we have discovered, help me identify friction in how I work. Look for activities that take longer than they should, require too many manual steps, are difficult to start, involve searching across multiple places, are easy to forget or regularly create rework. Ask me questions to test your assumptions. Then create a list of friction points and explain their likely causes.
Workshop steps
  1. Run the prompt
  2. Let Copilot test its assumptions
  3. Review the friction points and causes

Tip: Friction is where the biggest wins usually hide. Where do you lose momentum? What work do you delay starting?

Prompt 5Discovery

Prioritise my Copilot opportunities

Purpose Turn the discovery into a shortlist, not an overwhelming list of ideas.

Based on our discovery, identify five realistic opportunities where Copilot could help me work more effectively. For each opportunity, explain the problem, the current effort involved, how Copilot might help and the potential benefit. Prioritise the opportunities using these criteria: frequency, effort saved, usefulness and ease of getting started. Do not recommend a specific solution yet. Ask me to select one opportunity to explore further.
Workshop steps
  1. Run the prompt and pick one opportunity
  2. Record: the activity, the current problem, who it's for
  3. Record: the desired outcome and what makes a solution useful

Prompt 7Prompt or agent?

Help me choose the right approach

Purpose Connect your discovery to the right solution.

Help me decide the simplest effective Copilot approach for the opportunity I selected. Compare the following options: a one-time prompt, a reusable prompt, an existing Microsoft agent or a new personal agent. Ask me questions about how often I complete the activity, whether the instructions remain consistent, whether specific knowledge is required, whether other people need to use it and how repeatable the desired output is. Recommend an approach and explain your reasoning.
Workshop steps
  1. Run the prompt
  2. Answer Copilot's questions
  3. Read the recommendation — don't accept it straight away
  4. Ask the follow-up question below
Follow-up question
What assumptions did you make when recommending this option?
Quick reference: prompt vs reusable prompt vs agent

A prompt makes sense when…

  • The need is occasional
  • Context changes each time
  • One request covers it
  • No consistent instructions needed
  • You're still exploring the problem

A reusable prompt makes sense when…

  • The activity repeats
  • The request has a recognisable structure
  • You want similar output each time
  • You can add context when you run it

An agent makes sense when…

  • The purpose is stable
  • The same instructions apply repeatedly
  • It needs defined behaviour or boundaries
  • Specific knowledge should inform responses
  • Testing and refining adds ongoing value

Prompt 8Prompt or agent?

Challenge the agent idea

Purpose A safeguard before you start building.

Act as a critical reviewer of my proposed agent idea. Identify where I may be overcomplicating the solution, where a reusable prompt or existing capability might be sufficient and what needs to be true for an agent to provide ongoing value. Then give me a recommendation: proceed with an agent, simplify the idea or investigate an existing solution first. Explain your recommendation clearly.
Workshop steps
  1. Run the prompt
  2. Read Copilot's critique and recommendation

Tip: The goal isn't to force every idea into an agent — it's a thoughtful decision. If a reusable prompt suits better, great call. You can still follow the Agent Builder demo with a practice scenario.

Part 3 · Design your agent

Capture everything you've discovered and decided in one design document.

Prompt 9Design your agent

Create my agent design document

Purpose Turn your discovery and decisions into a practical design you can build from.

Based on everything we have discovered and decided in this conversation, help me create a practical design document for my agent. Use the information already captured, but do not fill gaps with assumptions. If an important design decision is still unclear, ask me one question at a time before completing the document. Structure the design document with: 1. Agent name 2. Purpose and problem it solves 3. Intended users 4. Tasks the agent should help with 5. What is in scope and out of scope 6. Instructions for how the agent should behave 7. Knowledge or information sources it should use 8. Expected response style and format 9. Three useful conversation starters 10. Example user requests 11. How we will test whether the agent works well Keep the document concise and practical. Clearly label anything that still needs to be confirmed. At the end, provide: • a complete agent design document in word format
Workshop steps
  1. Run the prompt in the same chat
  2. Answer Copilot's questions one at a time
  3. Check anything labelled as still to be confirmed
  4. Open and save the Word design document

Tip: Your design document becomes the blueprint for building your agent in Agent Builder.
